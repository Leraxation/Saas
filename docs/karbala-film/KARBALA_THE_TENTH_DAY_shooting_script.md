## PART 1: FILM OVERVIEW & ATMOSPHERE

### 1.1 Cinematic Style & Color Grading

**The look in one line:** a bleached, bone-white sun that has burned the color out of everything except blood.

| Act | Palette | Light | Lens / Movement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Journey & siege (0:00–0:50) | Desaturated ochre, dust-grey, ash. Skin tones leached. | Stark overhead sun, heat shimmer on every horizon. | Locked-off extreme wides, slow push-ins. Long-lens compression to show the enemy army as a wall. |
| Night of Ashura (0:50–1:10) | Cold indigo night, oil-lamp amber, firelight orange. | Single practical light sources. Starlight. | Still, intimate 50mm. Handheld only on faces. |
| Ashura (1:10–4:40) | Highest contrast of the film. Crushed blacks, blown highlights. Blood is the only saturated color: deep arterial crimson. | Midday sun directly overhead, no fill, hard black shadows under helmets. Dust turns the air to gold-grey gauze. | Handheld 35mm in combat, 45-degree shutter for crisp, brutal motion. Slow motion reserved for three moments only: the first volley, the waterskin pierced, the horse's return. |
| The Euphrates (3:06–3:18) | The one warm, golden, saturated sequence in the film. Water as temptation. | Low afternoon sun on moving water. | Steadicam, graceful. Then it is ripped away. |
| Aftermath (4:40–5:00) | Ash, smoke, a dimming red sun. | Backlit by burning tents. | Slow, drifting, elegiac. |

**Textures to insist on in every shot:** cracked lips, sweat tracks through dust on faces, rust-pitted lamellar and chainmail, oiled leather, sun-rotted linen, flies, hoof-churned sand, blood going black in the heat.

**Depiction note (director's decision, not a script change):** the script is written as a standard war epic with the Imam fully depicted. If you later want to honor the convention of not showing the faces of the Ahl al-Bayt, every Hussain shot below is framed so you can swap to Option B (backlit, over-the-shoulder, hands, or a halo of blown-out sunlight) without changing the edit. It also solves AI face-consistency problems.

### 1.2 Sound Design Notes

**Score.** No triumphal brass anywhere. The orchestra is strings and drums; the soul is a solo ney (reed flute) and a duduk.
- *Theme I, "Thirst"*: a single ney over silence, cracked and breathy. First heard at 0:46, last heard at 3:36.
- *Theme II, "The Standard"*: low cellos and basses in a slow ostinato, bass drums struck with felt, a wordless male choir. Belongs to Abbas; it is heard only when the standard is up.
- *Theme III, "Lament"*: a lone female voice, Arabic, unaccompanied. Heard exactly once: when the horse returns.
- *Pulse*: a daf (frame drum) as a heartbeat under the companions' stands, accelerating through the afternoon until it stops dead at 3:36.

**Ambient.** Constant wind and sand hiss. Tent canvas snapping, guy ropes creaking. Horses blowing, stamping, flies. The Euphrates is always faintly audible from the camp, a cruelty in the mix.

**Combat.** Steel on steel with hard, dry transients, no Hollywood ring. Arrows whistle then thud into wood, leather, flesh. Chainmail chimes. Hooves live in the subwoofer. Men's breathing close to the mic.

**Silence as a weapon.** All sound drops to nothing at three points: the lamp going out (0:54), the arrow at Ali al-Asghar (3:38), the horse appearing out of the dust (4:25).

**Voice.** The adhan at noon (2:09) is sung live, unaccompanied, cutting through the battle. Narration is low, unhurried, male, grave; never rushed to fit the picture. Arabic battle cries and the takbir are mixed under, not over.

**Settings.** Kling 3.0, 1080p, 16:9 (crop to 2.39:1 in the edit) or native 21:9 if offered. 10-second clips for duels and charges, 5-second clips for inserts and reactions. **Content-filter tactic.** Where a prompt describes a killing blow, the generator may refuse. Fallback for each is written as `[ALT]`: a reaction shot, a shadow, or the aftermath, which is also how real war films cut most deaths.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Shot count:** 53 generated shots, 10 of which have slow-motion inserts. At three to four takes per shot this is roughly 1,000 to 1,100 generated seconds, which matches the earlier cost estimate of $180 to $420 in generation credits.

**Order of generation.** Build the five hero character references first (Hussain, Abbas, Zaynab, Ali al-Akbar, Qasim), then the horse. Generate every tent-line and landscape establishing shot second, because they are cheap and set the grade. Generate the combat last, with the `[ALT]` versions ready for any refusal.

**Editing.** Cut the Ashura block to the daf; every death lands on a beat. Let the three silences run a full second longer than feels comfortable.

**Historical sources behind the beats.** The sequence of deaths, the trench of fire, Hurr's defection, Umar ibn Sa'd's first arrow, Umm Wahb and the tent pole, Sa'id al-Hanafi at the noon prayer, Ali al-Akbar's thirst, Qasim's sandal strap, Abbas pouring the water back and the loss of both arms, the arrow of Harmala, the three-pronged arrow, the count of wounds, the returning horse, and Zaynab shielding Ali Zayn al-Abidin from Shimr all follow al-Tabari's account (via Abu Mikhnaf) and the standard maqtal literature. Where accounts differ on who struck a particular blow, the script uses the most commonly cited name.
